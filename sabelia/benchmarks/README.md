# Benchmark

Generated by `scripts/benchmark_table.py` from each run directory's
`runs.jsonl`, with the paired ablation view from `scripts/ablation_table.py`.
Reproduce with:

```bash
.venv/bin/sabelia benchmark --dataset configs/datasets/synthetic-benchmark.yaml --out benchmarks/runs-3seeds --seeds 3 --epochs 12
.venv/bin/sabelia benchmark --dataset configs/datasets/ednet-kt1-sample.yaml   --out benchmarks/runs-ednet-fixed     --seeds 3 --epochs 20
.venv/bin/sabelia benchmark --dataset configs/datasets/duolingo-hlr-300k.yaml  --out benchmarks/runs-duolingo-fixed  --seeds 3 --epochs 20
```

The public datasets are not in this repository; the configs say where to get
them and under which licence.

## The correction of 2026-09-09

Every neural row published before this date is withdrawn. `predict_dataset`
scored only each learner's last `max_len` events and `predict` answered 0.5
for anything older, so neural models were compared with baselines **on
different events** — and the events they skipped are the early ones, where
the base rate is 0.565 against 0.700 later. The easy part was kept.

Both paths now walk a long learner in overlapping windows, and every model
answers for every event. The tables below are the re-measurement. It changed
the verdict in both directions: on EdNet the engine went from failing V1's
exit condition to clearing it, and its AUC there fell by 0.02.

## Synthetic — the pipeline, not the models

Simulated learners from `sabelia.simulation`: a Rasch-like process with mild
log-time forgetting. A model that wins here has learned that process. Nothing
about real learners follows from it, and sequences are shorter than the
window, so the correction above does not touch these numbers.

## The baselines that were missing, and the feature they exposed

Every baseline in this package modelled the *concept*. On EdNet a concept is
a tag over 12,056 distinct questions, and the question's own difficulty is
the dataset's strongest signal, so the field the engine was compared against
could not see what mattered. Three baselines that can now exist:
`item_mean` (one smoothed number per question), `logistic_features`
(eighteen causal features, Newton-solved) and `gradient_boosting` (the same
table, boosted).

Against them the engine as it stood lost by 0.10 AUC. The fix was not a
bigger model: the difficulty of the question being asked is knowable before
it is answered, and the model was never given it — the item reached it only
as a free embedding to discover from scratch. `use_difficulty` puts the
smoothed per-item and per-concept rates into the query.

That is by far the largest effect any change has had in this project — six
times the model's own seed spread — and it closes most of a gap that no
amount of attention machinery had closed. A transformer with a causal
encoder, a forgetting gate and 862k parameters could not extract from 12,056
free embeddings what a per-question average gives in three lines.

**Where it leaves the engine.** It is now second on AUC, behind a gradient
boosting over the same eighteen features (0.7456 against 0.7255), ahead of
the logistic regression on nothing — that one is 0.7323, still above it — and
it has the best calibration in the table (0.0101 ECE). It is also 600 times
slower to train than the logistic regression. V1's exit condition, read
against this field, is **not met**.

## What the three datasets say together

**Drop the item embedding.** It is the only ablation ever to move the same
way on every seed of a dataset — +0.0033 AUC on all three Duolingo seeds —
and on EdNet it is +0.0139, +0.0041 and −0.0001, so five of six seeds across
the two. It also removes most of the model: 90,257 parameters against 861,969
on EdNet. `use_item` now defaults to false.

**The other three are undecided everywhere.** Time, the forgetting gate and
the response-time channel each flip sign on at least one seed of every
dataset. EdNet removes the excuse that Duolingo lacked the signal: it records
response times and the model reads them, and removing them changes nothing
measurable.

**The blend is first and not adopted.** `stack` — Sabelia and BKT, logistic
weights fitted on validation — leads both real tables, because the two are
wrong in different places: Sabelia predicts a learner's first attempt at a
concept better, BKT everything after. It wins by 0.001 log loss over a single
model a tenth of the size, which does not pay for running two models in
production. It stays in the benchmark as the ceiling to beat.

**The neural model is the unsteady one.** Its seed spread on EdNet (±0.0138)
is twice the baselines'. Any future comparison needs more seeds than a
logistic model would to say the same thing.
