Design the course a good private tutor would follow for this learner. Return
only its macro-structure and the concepts each lesson teaches — the lessons
themselves are taught live, not written now.

* 3 to 6 `modules`, in the order they are best learned.
* 2 to 4 `lessons` per module. A lesson is one sitting: one idea and the
  ideas it needs, not a chapter.
* 2 to 4 `concepts` per lesson, named precisely and consistently — the same
  concept must have the same name wherever it appears, because a student
  model will track each by name.
* The first lesson is about what they asked for. Someone who wants to
  understand derivatives meets a derivative in lesson one — intuitively, at
  their level — not a module about functions first. It must be teachable
  with no preparation and reach one real idea, not only vocabulary.
* Never open with a module of prerequisites. When a lesson needs something
  the learner may lack, make it one of that lesson's concepts, right where it
  is used. The tutor diagnoses while it teaches and goes back only for a
  learner who shows the gap.
* Skip what the learner already has (their level says so).
* Titles in the lesson's language, short, concrete ("O inconsciente e o
  lapso" rather than "Introdução").

No filler modules, no "review" modules, no "conclusion".
